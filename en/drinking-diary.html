How to Keep a Drinking Diary & Alcohol Calendar

You only know how much you really drink once you write it down. Many people put their own consumption well below the real figure, not on purpose, but because a glass here and a beer there never add up in your head. A drinking diary, also called an alcohol diary or alcohol calendar, does that sum for you. This article gives you a free template to copy or download, a filled-in example and a clear way to read your entries over time.

What is a drinking diary?

A drinking diary is a simple day-by-day log across your calendar: every day you note what you drank and how much of it. Days without alcohol go in too. Doctors, counsellors and cut-down programmes have recommended keeping a drink diary for years because it starts without giving anything up. At first you change nothing, you just look closely.

The point isn't to catch yourself out. It's to get an honest number, so you can decide for yourself whether you want to change anything, and what.

A drinking diary answers "how much?". The question "why?" belongs in a separate journal, where you write down triggers, cravings and consequences. How that works is explained in Alcohol Cravings: Spot Your Triggers and Consequences in a Journal.

What goes into a drinking diary?

A few details per day are enough:

  • Date, including days without alcohol
  • Drink, for example beer, wine, prosecco or spirits
  • Amount in millilitres
  • Strength in % ABV (it's on the label)
  • Grams of alcohol, which you work out from amount and strength (see below)

How to work out grams of alcohol

A pint of beer and a glass of wine only become comparable once you convert both into grams of pure alcohol. The formula is simple:

Amount in ml × ABV in % ÷ 100 × 0.8 = grams of alcohol

The 0.8 is the density of alcohol: one millilitre of pure alcohol weighs about 0.8 grams. Half a litre of beer at 5% therefore contains 500 × 5 ÷ 100 × 0.8 = 20 grams of alcohol. If you think in UK units, one unit is 8 grams; a US standard drink is 14 grams. Values for common glasses are in the table in Am I Drinking Too Much?.

A filled-in example

This is what an ordinary week can look like:

Day Drink Amount % ABV Grams
Monday nothing 0 g
Tuesday 2 × beer 2 × 500 ml 5% 40 g
Wednesday nothing 0 g
Thursday wine 200 ml 12% 19 g
Friday 3 × wine 3 × 200 ml 12% 58 g
Saturday beer, 2 × spirits 330 ml, 2 × 20 ml 5%, 40% 26 g
Sunday nothing 0 g

That's 143 grams for the week, a little over 20 grams a day on average, with three days without alcohol. Friday alone brings 58 grams, almost as much as Tuesday and Thursday together.

How to read your drinking diary

A single week can mislead, because a week full of occasions and a quiet week look completely different. Keep your diary over a longer stretch, then look at three things:

  1. Your consumption per week. Add up the grams for each week and divide by 7. That gives you a daily figure you can compare with the guidelines. The UK guideline is no more than 14 units a week (about 16 grams a day), the US guideline 2 drinks a day for men and 1 for women (28 and 14 grams), and the World Health Organization stresses that less is always the lower risk.
  2. Your days without alcohol. Most health services advise several alcohol-free days a week. Count how many you had.
  3. Your peak days. On which days of the week does most of it add up? Often just a couple of evenings make up the bulk of the week, and that's where a closer look pays off most.

How your consumption compares with the average in your country is covered in Am I Drinking Too Much?.

Paper, spreadsheet or app?

All three work, though each comes with its own friction in everyday life.

Paper is the easiest way to start, and many doctors and counselling services hand out printed drink diaries. In everyday life the drawbacks show quickly: the notebook is rarely with you at a bar or dinner, and at the end of the week you are working out grams by hand.

A spreadsheet takes over the arithmetic, for example with our Excel template above, and works well at a desk. On a phone on Friday night it is clunky, so entries often end up being guessed from memory on Sunday.

An app on your phone is always in your pocket, so you log drinks at the moment you have them. What matters is that the alcohol tracker calculates real amounts of alcohol rather than simply counting glasses, where a small lager and a strong IPA count the same. Frequently asked questions

How long should I keep a drinking diary?
Long enough to cover both quiet weeks and weeks with parties, trips or stress. A single week tells you very little; only across several weeks do the ups and downs even out so your average becomes reliable. Many people simply keep going afterwards because logging takes only seconds.

Do I need to log the days without alcohol?
Yes. An empty day on your calendar can mean you didn't drink or that you forgot to log it. Only when sober days are logged explicitly is your average correct, and only then do you see how many alcohol-free days you really had.

Is there a free drinking diary template I can print or download?
Yes. You can copy or print the table above, or download our free Excel template. If you don't want to track on paper or in a spreadsheet, Relimie handles the logging and the maths on your phone, and the drinking diary is included in the free version.

Will my doctor or counsellor accept a diary from an app?
That depends on them, not on the app, so ask before you start which format they want. With Relimie Premium you can export your history as a PDF report; whether that report is accepted is up to the person you're working with.

What if I forgot a day?
Fill it in as well as you remember. A rough estimate is better than a gap, because a gap distorts your average more than an approximate figure does.
