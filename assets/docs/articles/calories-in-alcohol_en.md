# How Many Calories Are in Alcohol? Wine, Beer & Spirits Compared

When we think about what we eat and drink, alcohol is often the part that quietly slips off the list. A glass of wine after work, a beer with friends, a cocktail to round off dinner. These moments matter, and they deserve to be enjoyed. They also carry more calories than most people expect. Knowing the numbers has nothing to do with guilt. It just helps you see the full picture, so the choices you make stay yours.

## The quick answer

Pure alcohol contains about 7 calories per gram, nearly as much as pure fat (9 kcal/g) and almost double the calories in sugar or protein (4 kcal/g). On top of that, many drinks add sugar from mixers, juices and liqueurs.

Here is how common drinks compare. Values are approximate and depend on strength and serving size.

| Drink | Typical serving | Calories (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Dry white wine | 175 ml glass (12%) | ~140 kcal |
| Red wine | 175 ml glass (13%) | ~170 kcal |
| Large glass of wine | 250 ml (13%) | ~240 kcal |
| Prosecco or Champagne | 125 ml flute | ~90 kcal |
| Lager or ale | pint / 568 ml (5%) | ~215 kcal |
| Bottled beer | 330 ml (5%) | ~140 kcal |
| Spirit (gin, vodka, whisky) | single 25 ml (40%) | ~55 kcal |
| Gin and tonic | 25 ml plus regular tonic | ~120 kcal |
| Margarita or mixed cocktail | one glass | ~250 to 350 kcal |
| Cream liqueur | 50 ml | ~160 kcal |

## Why alcohol calories are so easy to miss

Nutritionists sometimes call these "empty calories": they give you energy but almost no vitamins, fibre or protein. A few things make them especially easy to overlook.

- Drinks don't feel like food. A pint can carry as many calories as a slice of pizza, but it doesn't register the same way.
- Mixers and cocktails hide sugar. The spirit itself may be modest, while the tonic, juice or syrup often is not.
- One drink leads to a nibble. Alcohol can lower your resolve, so the late-night snack is really part of the same round.

## How quickly it adds up

The individual numbers look small, so it helps to zoom out. Two 175 ml glasses of wine most evenings is roughly 320 calories a day, around 2,200 a week, or close to 9,500 a month. That is the calorie equivalent of a full extra day of eating every week, entirely from drinks.

Seeing that total isn't meant to alarm you. For many people it is simply the missing piece that finally makes their week add up.

## Five easy ways to cut liquid calories

1. Pour a smaller measure. A 125 ml glass instead of 250 ml halves the calories with barely any difference to the ritual.
2. Reach for a spritzer. Wine topped with soda, or a lower-strength option, keeps the taste and trims the total.
3. Alternate with water. One glass of water between drinks slows the pace and cuts the count.
4. Watch the mixer, not just the spirit. Slimline tonic or soda can save a hundred calories a glass.
5. Track before you trim. You can't adjust what you can't see, and a few logged days usually reveal the easiest win.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which alcohol has the fewest calories?**
Neat spirits with a calorie-free mixer such as soda water or slimline tonic tend to be lowest per serving, around 55 to 60 calories for a single measure. Among wines, a dry white is usually the lightest, thanks to its lower alcohol and minimal residual sugar. A small glass of it, or a light beer, is also relatively modest.

**Does wine or beer have more calories?**
Per glass it is close, but it depends on the pour. A pint of beer usually beats a small glass of wine because the serving is larger. Measure for measure, wine is more calorie-dense because it is stronger.

**Do the calories from alcohol really count?**
Yes. Your body uses the energy from alcohol just as it does from food, and because it burns alcohol first, other calories are more likely to be stored. That is why drinks belong in the picture, not outside it.
